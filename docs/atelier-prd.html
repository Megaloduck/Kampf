1. Product snapshot

Atelier is a desktop-first, local-first workspace for scholarly research. Its subject is the part of research that sits between collecting sources and drafting a paper: deciding what belongs, reading with a stated purpose, recording interpretation separately from source text, comparing sources, naming tensions, and assembling writing material. The product's public tagline states the thesis directly: "Research has a middle. Atelier gives it structure."

What the product claims about itself

Positioning statement (manual): "Atelier's job is to make research work inspectable." It brings together objects usually spread across browser tabs, reference managers, note apps, documents, spreadsheets and private memory: guiding inquiries, sources, reading intent, source notes, locators, synthesis, concepts, relations, open questions, preparation packets and review.

What it explicitly refuses to be

  • Not a paper generator, and no substitute for close reading or interpretation.
  • Not a continuous cloud collaboration platform or instructor portal.
  • Not a replacement for Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote or discipline databases.
  • Not a source-verification service — AI leads require independent checking.
  • Not a system that certifies quality, originality, privacy posture or readiness.
  • No claim of formal accessibility compliance.

3. Users, jobs and positioning

SegmentJob to be doneWhat Atelier provides for it
Researchers Maintain epistemic continuity across long projects: from a source passage to a note, a synthesis, a concept, and a writing plan. Traceable objects at every step; reverse context ("Used in"); passage return; project archives that restore.
Students Learn a reusable research method and make the development of an argument visible before the paper is due. The loop as an interface; source rationale; separation of source text from interpretation; fidelity and adherence checks.
Instructors Give feedback while thinking can still change, using process evidence and a practical rubric. Readable Dossier exports; Journey (8 stages) as process evidence; reflection surfaces in Review; teaching toolkit PDF.
Institutions Support coherent research teaching and integrity-by-demonstration with low-friction adoption and no central student-data platform. No accounts, no central store; per-project local workspaces; institution decides AI route and storage policy.

Competitive stance

Adjacent toolRelationship
Zotero / EndNote / MendeleySits beside them. Imports citation metadata; does not store or sync libraries, and does not format citations.
Reference managers + note apps (Obsidian, Notion)Atelier supplies the scholarly object model and method; it is not a general note tool.
PDF annotators (Zotero, Folio, Preview)Its Reader is document-level but the point is the note, the locator and what the note later supports.
AI writing assistantsDeliberate inverse: AI never writes the artefact; it returns named readings for the researcher to judge.

The differentiating idea

"Human interpretation first, AI second." This is not a slogan in the UI; it is enforced architecturally:

  • AI output never becomes a stored artefact without a human action.
  • AI never drafts synthesis notes, replacement sentences or paper text.
  • AI actions are named, enumerated and bounded, each returning a fixed-vocabulary reading.
  • Every action is previewable and recorded.

Integrity rules the model must enforce (observed as product claims, therefore requirements):
  • Every source note retains a locator or explicitly degrades to a nearest-safe fallback.
  • No relation is created from lexical or topical similarity; relations are researcher-created.
  • Row evidence is never presented as cell evidence.
  • No AI text becomes a stored artefact without an explicit human action.

7.4 Citation import

  • Sources: local Zotero application connection, citation export files (Mendeley, EndNote), pasted citations, DOIs.
  • Candidate review step before records are created; duplicate detection within the project.
  • Does not modify external libraries, copy attachments or annotations, or promise continuous sync.
  • Zotero failure path: open Zotero and enable local application access, or import an export file.

7.6 Source Notes

  • Note types supplied by the active research profile (discipline-aware vocabulary).
  • Fields: researcher interpretation, source location, origin, tags, attachments, optional AI checks.
  • The distinction between "what the source says" and "what I think" is structural, not stylistic.
  • Portable downstream: notes feed Matrix rows, Synthesis evidence, Trail, Preparation.

7.7 Research Trail

  • Traces a source to its analytical purpose, research contribution, supported concepts, and recorded synthesis uses.
  • Reverse context ("Used in") shows where a source note, synthesis, concept or question is relied on elsewhere.
  • This is the audit view of the project's reasoning, and the main answer to "why is this source here?".

7.9 Synthesis

  • Project-level interpretation, written over deliberately selected evidence.
  • Evidence types: source-note evidence, direct source locators, concepts, perspective tags, background sources.
  • Origin is retained: researcher, optional AI assistance, or imported material.
  • AI may assist but never produces the synthesis note itself.

7.10 Concepts & Relations

  • Concepts are independently meaningful framework nodes with alternate names, searchable via Go to…, showing "Appears in" context.
  • Connection builder offers exactly four labels: Supports, Adds tension, Challenges, Gives an example.
  • Relations carry a rationale when it matters; no automatic inference from shared words or similarity.

7.11 Open Questions

  • Statuses: Still open, Partly addressed, In preparation, Set aside.
  • List and Board views; evidence links and researcher notes; can be carried into a writing plan.
  • Exists to keep productive uncertainty visible rather than resolving it prematurely.

7.12 Preparation

StepWork
1. Choose materialSelect synthesis notes, source notes, sources, concepts, relations and questions.
2. Shape directionSet the plan's direction for a chapter, paper, meeting or presentation.
3. Review & exportCheck grounding, then export a focused Word plan/brief; optional Detailed outline (argument scaffold).

Choosing citations and locations deliberately is part of the step; writing still happens outside.

7.13 Project Review

  • Reflective prompts over evidence, structure and recent activity.
  • Recommends a reflection focus ("what needs revision or further inquiry").
  • Optional AI-assisted review returns a reading, not a score.
  • Produces no completion verdict by design.

8. AI assistance subsystem

8.1 Contract

  1. Initiated — every action is chosen by the researcher; nothing runs in the background.
  2. Bounded — each action declares the context it needs and sends only that.
  3. Previewable — a request preview can show exactly what would be sent, before it is sent.
  4. Judged, not applied — output is a reading from a fixed vocabulary; the researcher decides what to do.
  5. Recorded — an AI ledger records each request, its outcome, and the researcher's decision.

8.2 Action catalog

ActionMomentQuestion it answersVerdict vocabulary
Paraphrase Fidelity CheckWhile reading Compares the note with the passage selected: are the source's content and my interpretation still separable? Separable · Blended · Transcription
Reading Purpose AdherenceWhile reading Measures a session's notes against the stated reading purpose — did the source change what matters, or did attention drift? On purpose · Redirected · Diffuse
Source Grounding ObservationsWhile synthesizing / comparing Which concepts, synthesis notes, relations and preparation statements are visibly connected to sources, and which are not yet grounded? Visible grounding · Not yet visible · Relations needing rationale · Questions to consider
Further actions (names undisclosed)Reading (3 total), Synthesizing (3), Comparing (3), Deciding direction (4) The tour groups actions by moment and counts them: 3 + 3 + 3 + 4 = 13 actions in total. UNKNOWN — only three vocabularies are public

8.3 Hard exclusions (product-level, not prompt-level)

  • Never invents a quotation, page number, locator, date or bibliographic detail; where provenance is missing it says so and asks the researcher to verify first.
  • Never writes the synthesis note, the replacement sentence, or any part of the paper; it names the move that would be needed and stops.
  • Never treats recognition of a well-known work as evidence of what that work says.
  • Never sends full PDFs, attached files, unrelated projects, the whole workspace, local paths, API keys or app configuration automatically.

8.4 Routing and control

RouteMeaningNotes
Atelier built-inDefault managed service; no endpoint or API key needed.Third-party infrastructure incl. Google Gemini; no provider key stored in the app.
Your provider (BYOK)Researcher-supplied compatible endpoint and model.Connection must be verified before it becomes active.

Off switch: AI can be disabled entirely; the workspace then behaves as a purely manual tool. OBSERVED

8.5 Response language

  • One research-and-AI response language per project.
  • Research text may be multilingual with direction-aware presentation; interface labels stay English.
  • Model explanations are requested in the project's response language — not a translation guarantee for sources.

13. Interface and design observations

  • Two themes — a Paper and a Dark appearance, switched from the site and reflected in app screenshots ("Dark theme" variants of each tour screen).
  • Typography scale — a workspace-level setting, exposed in Settings → Workspace.
  • Screen capture convention — the tour presents ten screens "each shown complete in its natural aspect ratio", which implies dense, information-rich layouts rather than guided wizards. INFERRED
  • Card-based object presentation — source cards carry rationale, contributions and status; matrix rows carry evidence; review carries dimensions.
  • Method made visible — the interface repeatedly surfaces "where am I / what next": pathway on Project Home, next useful move, Journey stages, Review's recommended focus.
  • No chat surface — AI appears as named actions bound to objects rather than as a conversation. This is the single largest interface departure from mainstream AI products and should be preserved in any rebuild.
  • Documentation as product — a researcher manual, teaching toolkit, student handbook and eight task-based tutorials ship alongside the app.

15. Rebuild sequencing

A defensible build order that preserves the product's differentiating idea at every stage. Each milestone is independently useful.

MilestoneScopeWhy here
M1 — EncounterProject + inquiry, sources with rationale/intent/status, attachment, list and detail, local store, plain export.The rationale field is the cheapest differentiator: it makes the rest of the method meaningful.
M2 — EngageReader with locators, reading purpose, source notes with typed fields, note↔passage binding, enjoyable fallback.Without locators nothing downstream can be traced.
M3 — ChecksFidelity and adherence readings, deterministic first, model-optional; every reading logged.Proves the AI posture with the least machinery and no cloud dependency.
M4 — CompareLiterature Matrix with profile-aware columns, row evidence, CSV/XLSX export.Comparison is the first place separate sources become one argument.
M5 — StructureConcepts, four-label relations with rationale, open questions with statuses.The framework layer; deliberately last among synthesis pieces so it is earned by reading.
M6 — IntegratePreparation three-step plan, Detailed outline, Dossier/plan exports.Turns the workspace into an output without becoming a writing tool.
M7 — Continuity.atelier archive (Complete/Structure-only), workspace move, Go to…, Journey, Review.Trust and portability arrive once there is something worth losing.
Scope cuts that survive. Research profiles, collections, Zotero import, Board view, and the Journey export are all additive presentation or ingestion layers. Deferring them does not damage the core loop; deferring locators, rationale or the ledger would.
